What is AML Blockchain Checker?

AML Blockchain Checker is the most advanced multi-chain AML (Anti-Money Laundering) compliance verification platform in the crypto industry. It checks any wallet address or transaction across Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT TRC20, BNB BEP20, Solana, Polygon, and 14+ more blockchains for AML risk, sanctions exposure, mixer usage, and financial crime indicators — providing instant compliance reports starting from just $0.10 per check.

Which blockchains does AML Blockchain Checker support?

AML Blockchain Checker currently supports 20+ blockchains including: Bitcoin (BTC), Ethereum (ETH), USDT TRC20 (TRON), USDT ERC20, BNB / BEP20 (Binance Smart Chain), Solana (SOL), Polygon (MATIC), Avalanche (AVAX), Arbitrum, Optimism, Fantom, Cronos, Near, Algorand, Cardano (ADA), Litecoin (LTC), and more. How does AML Blockchain Checker calculate the AML risk score?

AML Blockchain Checker analyzes the complete on-chain transaction history of a wallet, traces fund flows up to 10 hops through the blockchain graph, checks the address and all counterparties against 15+ global sanctions lists (including OFAC, EU, UN, HM Treasury), detects mixer or tumbler exposure, scans for dark market and ransomware-linked activity, and computes a composite AML risk score from 0 (completely clean) to 100 (critical high risk). The scoring model weights 500+ individual risk indicators.

What is included in the AML compliance PDF report?

Every PDF report includes: the target wallet address and blockchain network, AML risk score (0–100), detailed risk category breakdown, sanctions check result (clear / direct match / indirect exposure), mixer and tumbler detection result, dark market exposure analysis, fund flow graph visualization, top counterparty risk summary, transaction volume statistics, report timestamp, and a signed compliance statement certifying the check was performed at that date and time.

Can AML Blockchain Checker detect Bitcoin mixer usage?

Yes. AML Blockchain Checker's mixer detection engine is trained on the full universe of known Bitcoin mixing services, including CoinJoin implementations (Wasabi Wallet, Whirlpool, JoinMarket), centralized Bitcoin tumblers, and chain-hopping obfuscation techniques. Mixer exposure is one of the highest-weighted risk indicators in the AML score. The detection covers both direct mixer transactions and downstream exposure where mixed funds appear multiple hops away from the target wallet.

Is AML Blockchain Checker aligned with FATF guidelines?

Yes. AML Blockchain Checker's risk methodology is fully aligned with FATF (Financial Action Task Force) guidance on virtual asset risk assessment, including the updated FATF Recommendations for virtual asset service providers (VASPs). The platform helps VASPs fulfill customer due diligence (CDD), enhanced due diligence (EDD), and Travel Rule obligations across all supported blockchains. Enterprise plans include dedicated FATF Travel Rule compliance workflow tools.

What is the difference between direct and indirect sanctions exposure?

Direct sanctions exposure means the checked wallet address appears directly on a sanctions list such as OFAC SDN. Indirect exposure means the wallet has transacted with a sanctioned entity within the analyzed transaction graph (up to 10 hops). Indirect exposure is classified by hop distance, transaction amount, and direction of fund flow, so the risk report clearly indicates whether the wallet received from, sent to, or merely co-interacted with a sanctioned counterparty, enabling proportional compliance decisions.

Can I check Ethereum DeFi wallets and smart contracts?

Yes. AML Blockchain Checker fully supports Ethereum wallet addresses (EOAs) and can analyze the transaction history of wallets that interact with DeFi protocols. For smart contract addresses, the platform provides counterparty risk analysis based on the wallets that have funded or interacted with the contract. AML screening for Ethereum Layer 2 networks (Arbitrum, Optimism, Base) is also supported, covering bridging activity and L2-native transactions.

What happens if my AML check returns a high-risk score?

If an AML check returns a high-risk score (60–100), the report provides a full breakdown of the specific risk factors — sanctions exposure, mixer usage, dark market links, or stolen fund connections. It is your responsibility as a business to make a compliance decision based on this information: refuse or reverse the transaction, request additional KYC/EDD from the counterparty, or file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with your jurisdiction's financial intelligence unit (FIU) as required by your local AML regulations.

Does AML Blockchain Checker protect user privacy?

AML Blockchain Checker only analyzes publicly available on-chain blockchain data — no private keys, passwords, or personal information are required or processed. The crypto addresses you submit for checking are public blockchain identifiers. Check results are stored securely and are only accessible to the authenticated account that ran the check. AML Blockchain Checker does not sell, share, or disclose check results or user data to third parties. All data is encrypted at rest and in transit per SOC 2 Type II standards.
